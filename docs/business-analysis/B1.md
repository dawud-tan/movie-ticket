# B1: should the booking platform continue? A brief for a one-session business analysis

A reusable brief for deciding whether a one-person software business should keep going. Part 1
is the founder's own numbers, filled in before any analysis. Part 2 is the prompt that turns them
into [`model.xlsx`](model.xlsx) and [`memo.md`](memo.md), both beside this file.

**Every number in Part 1 is a randomized example.** The values were drawn within reasonable ranges
from a real run, and they describe no real business, person or cinema. The setup is a regional
cinema chain (here 33 branches) piloting the platform at one branch, then paying a flat fee per
screen. Replace the values with your own, or edit the blue cells in `model.xlsx` directly.

Mark each value with its source: **[tree]** taken from a document in this repo or your agreement
draft, **[public]** a published price or regulation, **?** your own estimate or decision.

### Stop rules (decide these before you see the result)

- The most cash you are willing to lose in total, not counting hours: **Rp14.000.000**.
- The month by which a branch must be paying the monthly fee: **month 12** (December 2027). The
  pilot fee does not count.
- Stop pitching the chain if the pilot agreement is not signed, with the first instalment received,
  by **month 4**, or at once if they want the pilot free.
- Stop if free hosting is lost before revenue covers a VPS.
- Stop if you spend more than **70 h/month for three months running** while revenue is under
  **Rp2.200.000/month** (one branch's fee).

## Part 2: the prompt (start a session with "Run docs/business-analysis/B1.md, Part 2")

Your task is to decide whether the owner should keep running the booking platform, using the
numbers in Part 1. There is no ledger in the tree, so Part 1 is the only financial source. Do not
infer numbers from the code. If a Part 1 row is `?` and its value would change the answer, ask the
owner once, listing every such row together.

Read only these: Part 1; the fee, term and termination clauses of the pilot agreement, if one is
drafted; the pitch milestones, if written down; and §1 of `docs/pse-oss-registration.md`. Do not
read the code, because none of this is a codebase question.

Write the output beside this file. If the numbers are real and private, write it outside the repo
instead and commit nothing.

1. `model.xlsx`, with live formulas so the owner can change an input later without a session:
   - Inputs: the values from Part 1.
   - Unit economics per tenant: revenue, variable cost and contribution margin. Add CAC and payback
     if pitching costs are given.
   - Break-even: the number of tenants needed to cover fixed cash costs, both with and without the
     owner's time.
   - A 24-month cash forecast for each of the three cases: monthly burn, runway and the month the
     cash runs out.
   - Sensitivity: a small table of break-even tenants against price.
   - Stop-rule check: whether each rule from Part 1 is met, in each case.
2. `memo.md`, one page: the recommendation (continue / continue with conditions / stop), the
   numbers behind it, and which Part 1 assumption would reverse it.
3. A section of the memo on stopping under Indonesian law. Check each point against the statute
   text by web search and cite the Pasal; do not work from memory:
   - voluntary dissolution and liquidation of a PT Perorangan (PP 8/2021) when there are no unpaid
     debts;
   - PKPU (UU 37/2004 Pasal 222) and pailit (Pasal 2(1)) when there are;
   - personal liability of the director and shareholder (UU 40/2007 Pasal 3(2), Pasal 104);
   - that until the PT exists, the owner signs agreements as an individual;
   - in a wind-down, the customers' unrefunded payments and the obligations to tenants.

   Label this section as orientation, not legal advice.

Finish by giving the owner both file paths and the recommendation in one line.
